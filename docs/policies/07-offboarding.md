# Offboarding and Asset Return

Owner: IT Infrastructure · Version 2.8 · Effective 2025-09-01

## Return deadline

When you leave the company, all assets are returned on or before your last
working day. For remote employees, assets are shipped back using the prepaid
label IT Infrastructure provides, and the tracking number is given to IT on the
last working day.

## What must be returned

Every asset recorded against the employee in the asset register must be returned
when they leave, including laptops, mobile handsets, monitors, scanners and any
loaner units. Consumable peripherals such as keyboards, mice and headsets do not
need to be returned and are not tracked.

## The return process

1. IT Infrastructure produces the employee's asset list from the register one
   week before the last working day.
2. The employee and their manager confirm the list is complete. Anything missing
   from the list must be flagged at this point.
3. Assets are handed over and their condition recorded.
4. Each asset is either returned to spare stock as **Available**, or marked
   **Retired** where its condition or age warrants it.
5. The register is updated the same day. An employee record with assets still
   assigned to it blocks the final clearance.

## Data on returned devices

Devices are wiped and re-imaged before re-allocation. Employees are responsible
for moving their own work to approved company storage before their last day;
personal data on a returned device is deleted during the wipe and cannot be
recovered afterwards.

## Assets not returned

Where an asset is not returned by the last working day, IT Infrastructure
notifies HR and the reporting manager. The value of the outstanding asset may be
recovered from the final settlement in line with the employment agreement.

## Transfers during notice period

Assets may be transferred from a departing employee to a colleague before the
last working day, following the standard transfer procedure with both managers'
approval. This is preferred over returning a device to stock and re-issuing it,
which takes longer and adds a re-imaging cycle.
